# Proletarianization

← [Back to glossary](/glossaire/)

**Origin**: Marx, via Bernard Stiegler

---

## Definition

For Marx, proletarianization designates the loss by the worker of their relationship to the means of production: in the manufactory, the worker uses the tool; in the factory, the worker serves the machine. Stiegler extends and displaces this concept well beyond the working class: proletarianization is not pauperization — it is the loss of knowledge (know-how, savoir-vivre, the capacity to think). In the 21st century, technicians, intellectual professions, and consumers have all been proletarianized.

When a know-how embodied in an individual is captured and externalized in a machine, the individual loses the ability to put it into practice. They no longer "know" in the operative sense of the term: they may still possess abstract knowledge, but they have lost the concrete faculty of transforming it into action.

---

## In my writings

I apply this concept to "intellectual" or service professions. Proletarianization does not mean that one loses one's knowledge: it means that the locus where reasoning takes place migrates from the mind to the technical device. One shifts from producer of reasoning to verifier, from the one who thinks to the one who validates what a machine has produced.

This dispossession is particularly difficult to perceive because it is accompanied by an apparent gain in efficiency, speed, and volume. This is the double trap of the pharmakon: the poison presents itself in the guise of the remedy.

Proletarianization is never merely an individual decision. It results from an evolution of the technical milieu that, by transforming professional standards, renders certain know-how obsolete and others indispensable. When an entire professional milieu adopts AI and this transforms expected timelines, practiced pricing, and quality criteria, the person who refuses is not making a free choice: they are suffering a systemic constraint imposed by the transformed milieu.
